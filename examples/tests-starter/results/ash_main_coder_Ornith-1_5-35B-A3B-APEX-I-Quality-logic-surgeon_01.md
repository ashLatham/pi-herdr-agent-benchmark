The surgeon is the boy's mother.

The statement "he is my son" combined with the fact that the man (the father) is already established as present means the surgeon is a second parent. The common assumption that leads people astray is assuming surgeons (and parents) must be male. In reality, the surgeon is the boy's mother — a woman. (A secondary possibility would be that the "man" referenced is not the biological father but another parent/guardian, but the intended answer is that the surgeon is the mother.)
